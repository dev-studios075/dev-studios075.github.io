# Fleet Departure Management Checklist: What to Verify Before Gate-Out

Gate-out is the last point at which a problem is cheap to fix. Before the vehicle leaves, a missing document costs minutes. After it leaves, the same gap can mean a roadside penalty, a detained vehicle or a missed delivery window. **Departure management** is the discipline of making sure every trip is genuinely ready before it moves.

This checklist is for dispatchers, yard supervisors and branch managers. Print it, adapt it to your operation or build it into your transport management system as a required step before gate-out.

## How to Use This Checklist

- Run it at a set time before planned departure, not when the truck is meant to leave.
- Give one named person responsibility for confirming each section.
- Treat any unchecked critical item as a hold. The trip does not depart until it is resolved or consciously overridden by a manager.
- Record the result so you can review departure performance later.

## Section 1: Trip Verification

- Trip is created and confirmed with a unique reference
- Origin, destination and all stops are correct
- Pickup and delivery windows are recorded and achievable from the planned departure time
- Load description, weight and volume match the order
- Customer name and contact details are on the trip
- Special instructions are recorded
- Planned departure time is confirmed

## Section 2: Vehicle Readiness

- Correct vehicle assigned for the load type and size
- Vehicle is physically at the departure point
- Registration certificate (RC) valid
- Fitness certificate valid for the full trip duration
- Insurance valid for the full trip duration
- PUC certificate valid
- Permits valid for every state or zone on the route
- No overdue or pending maintenance
- Tyres, brakes, lights, indicators and mirrors checked
- Fuel or charge sufficient for the planned leg, with margin
- Tool kit, spare tyre, safety triangle, fire extinguisher and first-aid kit present
- Tracking device powered and reporting

## Section 3: Driver Readiness

- Correct driver assigned and confirmed
- Driver is present, fit for duty and not fatigued
- Driving licence valid and appropriate for the vehicle class
- Driving hours and rest requirements allow the full planned duty window
- Driver briefed on route, stops, timings, delivery instructions and escalation contacts
- Driver has the app or device needed for status updates and proof of delivery
- Advance, cash or fuel card issued as planned
- Driver knows the procedure for breakdown, accident or delay

## Section 4: Load and Loading

- Load is physically ready and matches the order
- Quantity and condition verified at loading
- Load is secured, balanced and covered as required
- Total weight is within the legal gross vehicle weight limit
- Seals applied and seal numbers recorded
- Hazardous, fragile or temperature-sensitive loads handled to their requirements
- Loading completed within the planned slot

## Section 5: Documentation

- Invoice or delivery challan prepared
- E-way bill generated where applicable, valid for the route and duration
- Lorry receipt or consignment note issued
- Customer-specific paperwork included
- FASTag account active and funded
- Copies of vehicle and driver documents in the cab
- Document details match the trip, consignor, consignee and vehicle number

## Section 6: Operational Tasks

- Gate pass or exit approval obtained
- Pre-trip vehicle inspection completed and recorded
- Trip status updated to "ready" in the system
- Customer or consignee notified of expected arrival where required
- Next-stop and return-load plans noted
- Open exceptions logged and approved

## Section 7: Gate-Out Confirmation

- All critical items above confirmed
- Actual departure time recorded
- Odometer reading recorded
- Tracking confirmed live after departure
- Departure delay, if any, recorded with a reason
- Trip status changed to "in transit"

## Critical Items: Never Depart Without These

Some items are hard stops, not warnings:

- Valid vehicle documents covering the whole trip
- A licensed, rested driver with enough driving hours
- A valid e-way bill where one is required
- A load within legal weight limits
- A working tracking device

If any one is missing, the vehicle holds until it is fixed.

## Common Reasons Trips Fail the Checklist

| Reason | Where to fix it |
| --- | --- |
| Documents prepared late or not at all | Prepare them when the trip is created |
| Vehicle returning behind schedule | Plan return times into the schedule |
| Driver assigned too late to be briefed or rested | Set an assignment cut-off |
| Load not ready at the dock | Align loading slots with departure times |
| Document expiry noticed only at the gate | Use expiry alerts |

If the same reasons keep recurring, fix the process that produces them instead of relying on the checklist to catch them every time. ## Turning the Checklist Into a System

A paper checklist is a good start, but it can be skipped, lost or filled in after the fact. Building it into your transport management system makes it far stronger:

- **Required gating:** the trip cannot move to "in transit" until required items are confirmed.
- **Automatic checks:** document validity and driver hours are verified by the system, not by memory.
- **Early alerts:** upcoming expiries and unassigned trips are flagged days or hours ahead.
- **Audit trail:** every departure records who confirmed what, and when.
- **Performance data:** planned and actual departure times and delay reasons feed continuous improvement.

## Review and Adapt

No checklist is perfect on day one. Review it monthly. Add items that caused real delays, remove ones that never matter and tailor it by route type, cargo type or customer. The best departure checklist is the one your team uses every time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is departure management?

It is the process of verifying that a trip, its vehicle, driver, load and documents are all ready before the vehicle leaves the yard or branch.

### When should the checklist be run?

At a set time before planned departure, early enough that any gap can still be fixed without delaying the trip.

### Who should complete it?

Dispatch usually owns the overall check, with yard staff, drivers and documentation confirming their own sections.

### What should happen if an item fails?

Hold the trip until it is resolved. Critical items should not be overridden without manager approval, and every override should be recorded.

### Can I use the checklist on paper?

Yes, but building it into your system makes it harder to skip and gives you a record to analyse.

### How often should I update it?

Review it monthly and add any item that caused a real delay or compliance problem.
